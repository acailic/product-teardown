# Teardown: Termly — legal paperwork as a per-website rent, metered by banner views

- **Date analyzed:** 2026-11-13
- **Category:** B2B/B2C · compliance SaaS (policy generators, consent management, DSAR)
- **Model:** per-website subscription (free → Starter → Pro+) with usage/feature gates; volume licenses and agency reseller program; owned inside the group.one brand family
- **Prices as seen (2026-11-13):** Free $0 (1 basic policy, 10,000 banner views/mo, quarterly scans) · Starter $10/website/mo billed annually ($14 monthly): 2 policies, 10 edits, 50,000 banner views/mo · Pro+ $15/website/mo annual ($20 monthly): unlimited policies, edits and banner views, weekly scans, Google Consent Mode v2, IAB TCF 2.3 · Agency custom; bulk discount from 5+ licenses (base $15/$20 per license shown) ([pricing](https://termly.io/pricing/))
- **Revenue evidence:** **estimated** — no public revenue figures; verified: founded 2017, Seattle, ~26 employees ([LinkedIn](https://www.linkedin.com/company/termly/)), listed as a group.one brand ([group.one](https://group.one/en/brands/))

## 0. The essence (ELI9)

Termly writes the boring legal pages every website is supposed to have — privacy policy, cookie notice, terms — and lends you the "accept cookies?" popup too. Laws say you must publish these pages and keep them current, but a lawyer costs thousands; Termly asks a few questions and writes them for you, free to start. Once your site grows — more popup views, more pages — you pay $10–20 a month per website, like renting insurance against paperwork. Over two million businesses have used it (says Termly), and it sits in the same family as WP Rocket and one.com, so their customers get recommended it too.

## 1. What it is, and for whom

Termly is an all-in-one privacy-compliance suite: ~15 policy generators (privacy, terms, cookie, disclaimer, EULA, refund, shipping…), a consent manager with script auto-blocking and consent logging, a cookie scanner, DSAR forms, and regulation monitoring covering "28 different data privacy laws" ([pricing](https://termly.io/pricing/), [site](https://termly.io/)). It sells itself as an IAB TCF 2.3 CMP and "Google Certified CMP Partner with Gold status" (vendor claim, [LinkedIn](https://www.linkedin.com/company/termly/)), with EU data storage on all plans. The primary user is the small-business owner or marketer with no legal team; agencies second. Termly Inc sits inside the group.one family, which independently lists it among its brands ([group.one](https://group.one/en/brands/)).

## 2. How it earns

**Per-website subscription gated by three meters** ([pricing, seen 2026-11-13](https://termly.io/pricing/)):

- **Meters:** banner views 10K→50K→unlimited, policies/edits 1→2+10→unlimited, scans quarterly→monthly→weekly turn site growth into upgrades and "staying compliant" renewals.
- Advertiser table stakes — Consent Mode v2, TCF 2.3, multi-language, regional rules, logo removal — are Pro+ gates at $20/mo, iubenda's Advanced band ([sibling teardown](2026-11-09-iubenda.md)).
- **Volume and partners:** bulk discounts from 5 licenses, an agency program with "volume based discounts starting at 50% off retail" ([agencies](https://termly.io/agencies/)), and a partner API for embedding scanners, banners and policies into other apps ([API launch](https://termly.io/updates-press/api-feature/)).
- A promo banner advertising "CYBERWEEK26" 50% off appeared only in the page's JavaScript layer; the static page shows FALL2026 at 20% off — either way, a discount-driven self-serve cadence ([pricing](https://termly.io/pricing/)).

**Revenue: estimated** — none published. Scale anchors are vendor claims ("2 million+ businesses helped," [site](https://termly.io/)) around a verified ~26-person team inside a hosting conglomerate.

## 3. Who the customers are

- **SMB and e-commerce site owners** — the free entry and content aimed at them: FAQs like "Do I need a disclaimer for my mom blog?" and "How do I create a shipping policy on Shopify?" describe Etsy/Shopify sellers, blogs and affiliate sites, not enterprises ([FAQ](https://termly.io/faq/)).
- **Ad-running sites with EU traffic** — regional consent rules, TCF 2.3 and Consent Mode v2 gates target businesses keeping Google Ads alive in the EEA ([pricing](https://termly.io/pricing/)).
- **Agencies and resellers** — a program to "manage hundreds of clients directly from one dashboard," with bulk import and white-label marketing materials ([agencies](https://termly.io/agencies/)).
- **The group.one install base** — cross-sold to one.com hosting customers and the WP Rocket crowd ([group.one](https://group.one/en/brands/)).
- Trustpilot 4.7/5 "Excellent" (431 reviews), reviews dominated by support interactions rather than legal substance ([Trustpilot](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/termly.io), seen 2026-11-13).

## 4. Why they use it

The job: **become and stay legally compliant without a lawyer.** Laws require the documents; platforms (Google Ads, app stores) increasingly require working consent. Alternatives: a template that goes stale, a lawyer at "thousands in legal fees" ([site](https://termly.io/)), or sibling CMPs — iubenda meters pageviews at €5–80/site, CookieYes and Cookiebot play the same band ([teardown](2026-11-09-iubenda.md)). Termly's funnel is generous at the start: generate the policy free, embed it, pay when traffic or breadth demands. Users stay because policies and banner are embedded everywhere, consent logs accumulate, and $10–20/mo hardly justifies ripping it out. The 2018 HN launch also shows the buyer this model loses: "quite impressed," but walked away because policies were embed-only — "relying on Termly to always work as it should" ([HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=17608530)).

## 5. What I would build better

1. **Sell ownership of the legal pages, not a hotlink.** The 2018 objection — embed-only delivery makes Termly a single point of failure for a site's load-bearing pages — still stands: "HTML embeddable policies" remains a Free-tier bullet ([pricing](https://termly.io/pricing/), [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=17608530)). A signed, static, self-hostable export with version history would convert exactly the buyer who said he "would have happily paid" — and price the trust a compliance vendor should own.
2. **Make consent conversion-safe, and market it to the marketer.** Google Consent Mode v2 is buried as a Pro+ checkbox ([pricing](https://termly.io/pricing/)), yet for an SMB running EEA ads the real fear isn't the fine — it's conversion reporting collapsing when consent drops. A "compliance that doesn't cost you conversions" dashboard (consent rates by region, modeled-conversion recovery, A/B tests) shifts the buyer to the performance marketer with budget — the trust-to-growth wedge Tally is opening ([teardown](2026-11-05-tally.md)).
3. **Bridge the "not a law firm" gap with a paid attorney-review SKU.** Termly markets "attorney-crafted" documents next to a footer disclaimer that it "is not a lawyer or a law firm" ([HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=17608530), [FAQ](https://termly.io/faq/)) — a tension first-time buyers notice. A flat-fee human review add-on (LegalZoom's playbook) monetizes the exact trust deficit the disclaimer creates and gives the support-heavy Trustpilot channel something legal to praise.
