# PDFShift — HTML to PDF, as a service

*Product teardown, 2026-11-05. All sources are free and public; every claim carries its link.*

## The essence (for a 9-year-old)

Imagine you built a beautiful page on the computer and need to send it to a customer as a printable "PDF" file, but computers often mess up the printing. PDFShift is a printing shop on the internet: you send it your page, and in about two seconds it sends back a perfect PDF. Programmers pay a small amount every month, like a magazine subscription, and every page they print uses a little of what they paid for. One man in France built this printing shop by himself, and today it pays him a salary.

## What the product is, and for whom

[PDFShift](https://pdfshift.io/) is a developer API that converts HTML documents or URLs into PDF files — and, more recently, into screenshots (JPG/PNG/WEBP). One REST call (`POST https://api.pdfshift.io/v3/convert/pdf`) returns a PDF rendered by a real browser engine; the founder says it runs Python (Sanic) with Pyppeteer, a port of Puppeteer ([homepage](https://pdfshift.io/), [founder on Hacker News](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49658230)). The site advertises 56,000+ registered developers, 86M+ conversions, ~1.5 s average conversion and 99.99% uptime ([homepage](https://pdfshift.io/)). It targets developers at product companies first, and since ~2024 also no-code and AI-agent builders, shipping an MCP server and one-click integrations for n8n, Zapier, Make, Bubble, Clay, Lovable and Base44 ([agents page](https://pdfshift.io/agents/)).

## How it earns

Subscription SaaS metered in "credits": one credit = one converted document, counted per 5 MB of output ([pricing page](https://pdfshift.io/pricing/)). Every paid plan includes all features — no feature gating ([founder on Hacker News](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=44200762)). Monthly tiers: Free (50 credits), Starter $9 (500), Boost $24 (2,500), Growth $39 (5,000), Business $99 (25,000), then Shift plans from $149 (50k) up to $999 (1M), with custom deals above; yearly billing gives two months free ([pricing page](https://pdfshift.io/pricing/)). Optional overage costs fall from $0.04 to $0.001 per extra credit as plans grow; the [FAQ](https://pdfshift.io/faq/) states the average customer uses 1,250 credits/month — the $24 Boost plan.

Actual revenue is unknown: it is a private company with no public books. Best public signals are founder-reported: $3.5K MRR in Jan 2020, $6.5K in Apr 2021, then a ~2-year plateau at ~300 customers and ~$7.5K MRR, a failed 2023 acquisition ($500K offered, cut to $400K, deal collapsed), ~$12K MRR stated by the founder in June 2025 ([Starter Story interview](https://www.starterstory.com/stories/pdfshift), [Hacker News](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=44200762)); a third-party profile reported $14K MRR in Aug 2024 ([Superframeworks](https://superframeworks.com/blog/pdfshift)).

## Who the customers are

Segments, with evidence from PDFShift's published testimonials ([pricing page](https://pdfshift.io/pricing/)) and its own marketing:

1. **SaaS/backend developers** generating documents for their end users — invoices, receipts, data exports. "We needed to export user data into a good readable format and send it via e-mail to our customers… most packages or services are just not that good" (Jordan K.); a PHP team replaced another API after "8 days with trial and error" (Amit G.).
2. **Agencies and marketing shops**: "I've been able to build non-core business tools that serve as lead generation… one of our most talked about offerings" (Greg P.).
3. **Print/production users**: high-resolution PDFs "with crop marks and bleed" for professional printing (Paul G.).
4. **No-code/automation builders**: the [agents page](https://pdfshift.io/agents/) sells invoices generated from payment webhooks, scheduled reports, contract automation and certificates at scale via n8n/Zapier/Make/Clay.

## Why they use it instead of alternatives

The job to be done: *"turn the HTML my app already generates into a print-perfect, brandable PDF at scale — without maintaining a browser farm."* The concrete alternatives:

- **DIY headless Chrome** — [Puppeteer](https://github.com/puppeteer/puppeteer) (~95.8k stars) or [Gotenberg](https://github.com/gotenberg/gotenberg) (~13.5k stars), both free: you pay zero license but own the fleet — fonts, security updates, crashes, scaling. Customers explicitly fled tools that "require a desktop environment (instead of a headless server)" or support only old CSS ([pricing page](https://pdfshift.io/pricing/)).
- **[DocRaptor](https://docraptor.com/)**, the upmarket rival: built on the Prince engine, it advertises SOC 2 + HIPAA compliance and accessible/tagged PDFs. PDFShift counters on simplicity and price-per-document, with credit cost falling to $0.001 at volume and all features on every plan.

Even acquisition is documented: ~46% of traffic is organic search, and the founder discovered his 2021–2023 plateau started when he stopped Google Ads; restarting them in Feb 2024 restored 8–9% monthly customer growth — developers search "convert HTML to PDF" exactly when the need hits ([Starter Story](https://www.starterstory.com/stories/pdfshift), [Superframeworks](https://superframeworks.com/blog/pdfshift)).

## What I would build better

1. **Tagged, accessible PDF output (WCAG / Section 508 / ISO-14289).** DocRaptor markets exactly this as what "other APIs can't match" ([docraptor.com](https://docraptor.com/)), and it is a hard requirement for government, education and regulated buyers. Shipping accessibility tagging on the Chrome-based engine would open a segment PDFShift currently cannot even sell to — the clearest upmarket wedge.
2. **A visual test sandbox with regression diffs.** Let developers render a template, preview page breaks, headers and footers as thumbnails, and diff renders across engine updates. The most-cited onboarding pain is fidelity trial-and-error ("8 days with another api"); a sandbox converts that pain into activation, cuts support load, and moves free accounts to paid faster.
3. **True pay-per-document billing with no monthly floor.** The average customer uses only half of the Boost quota, and spiky users (one quarterly invoice run) face a $9+ jump from the free 50 credits ([FAQ](https://pdfshift.io/faq/)). A metered $0.02–0.04/document option would capture hobbyists and seasonal users without touching the predictable subscriptions larger customers prefer.
