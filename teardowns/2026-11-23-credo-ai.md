- **Date analyzed:** 2026-11-23
- **Category:** B2B · enterprise SaaS (+ advisory services)
- **Model:** quote-based annual enterprise subscriptions + forward-deployed advisory services; demo-gated (no pricing page)
- **Prices as seen (2026-11-23):** none published — [/pricing returns 404](https://www.credo.ai/pricing) and every CTA is "Get a Demo." Market reports (third-party, competitor-sourced): **~$30K–$150K+/year** contracts, **$40K–$200K+** estimated first-year cost with implementation ([CO-AIMS review, 2026-02-01](https://co-aims.com/blog/credo-ai-review-2026-compliance-officers); echoed by [Execlave](https://www.execlave.com/blog/execlave-vs-credo-ai)) — *estimates, not official*
- **Revenue evidence:** **estimated** — no ARR published. Verified funding: $5.5M seed, Oct 2021 ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2021/10/19/credo-ai-launches-out-of-stealth-with-5-5-million-to-help-companies-with-ethical-ai/)) + $12.8M Series A led by Sands Capital, 2022 ([company announcement](https://www.credo.ai/news/credo-ai-announces-12-8-million-series-a-funding-round-for-responsible-ai)) = $18.3M total tracked ([Craft.co](https://www.craft.co/credo-ai)); [backers page](https://www.credo.ai/company/aboutus) now also lists FPV and Mozilla Ventures (round not publicly announced)

## 0. The essence (ELI9)

Credo AI sells big companies a control room for their AI: one list of every AI system and AI vendor the company uses (including the sneaky ones nobody reported), automatic risk scores, and ready-made rulebooks matching the new AI laws. Banks, insurers, and governments pay — an estimated $30,000 to $150,000 a year, since no price is published — because laws like the EU AI Act now make them prove their AI is safe, and doing that by hand takes months. It's a bit like a building inspector who moves into the building full-time: the safety paperwork never stops, so the rent never stops either. The founder started selling "responsible AI" two years before the strictest laws existed — and was first in line when they arrived.

## 1. What it is, and for whom

Credo AI is an enterprise AI governance platform: an [AI Registry](https://www.credo.ai/product) with shadow-AI discovery, continuous risk intelligence, and a policy engine whose "policy packs" map to the EU AI Act, NIST AI RMF, ISO 42001, and US rules ([homepage](https://www.credo.ai/)), plus GAIA, an AI assistant for governance intake, and [Agent Governor](https://www.credo.ai/agent-governor), a runtime-enforcement layer still labeled "Research Preview." It integrates with Snowflake, Databricks, AWS, Azure, ServiceNow, Jira, Slack, GitHub, and MLflow ([homepage](https://www.credo.ai/)) and ships Python/TypeScript SDKs ([docs.sdk.credo.ai](https://docs.sdk.credo.ai/)). The primary user is not an engineer but a central governance/risk function — Credo pitches a "translation engine" aligning data science, product, and compliance ([Series A post](https://www.credo.ai/news/credo-ai-announces-12-8-million-series-a-funding-round-for-responsible-ai)). Founded March 2020 by Navrina Singh, an ex-Qualcomm, ex-Microsoft PM, with a team of 15 at the October 2021 launch ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2021/10/19/credo-ai-launches-out-of-stealth-with-5-5-million-to-help-companies-with-ethical-ai/)). It has outgrown the small-business core; the early-story lesson earns the slot: **build the category for a regulation that doesn't exist yet, so you're the incumbent the day it passes.**

## 2. How it earns

A separate line sells [Advisory Services](https://www.credo.ai/advisory) — "Forward-Deployed AI Governance Experts" — services revenue wrapped around the platform. **Revenue: undisclosed.** The verified money events are funding rounds: $5.5M seed from Decibel, Village Global and AI Fund ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2021/10/19/credo-ai-launches-out-of-stealth-with-5-5-million-to-help-companies-with-ethical-ai/)); $12.8M Series A led by Sands Capital ([company announcement](https://www.credo.ai/news/credo-ai-announces-12-8-million-series-a-funding-round-for-responsible-ai)); $18.3M total tracked ([Craft.co](https://www.craft.co/credo-ai)). Recognition spend is heavy: [Forrester Wave Leader 2025](https://www.credo.ai/recognition/forrester-wave-2025), a 2025 Gartner AI TRiSM Market Guide mention ([company news](https://www.credo.ai/news/credo-ai-featured-in-2025-gartner-market-guide-for-ai-trust-risk-and-security-management-ai-trism)), Fast Company #6 Applied AI 2026 ([vendor blog](https://www.credo.ai/blog/credo-ai-named-to-fast-companys-annual-list-of-the-worlds-most-innovative-companies-of-2026)) — the analyst-validated enterprise motion.

## 3. Who the customers are

- **Regulated financial and insurance giants** — Mastercard's chief data officer: "Features like AI Registry and Vendor Registry have allowed us to maintain control of all AI use cases" (vendor-quoted, [customers page](https://www.credo.ai/customers), which carries a named Mastercard case study); Verisk's global CDO on scaling governance with the platform (same page).
- **Big tech and data platforms** — IBM's watsonx GM and Databricks' Roger Murff quotes; Amazon, Databricks, Northrop Grumman homepage logos — all vendor claims.
- **US federal / defense** — a Booz Allen Hamilton alliance "to bring Responsible AI to the US federal government" ([company news, 2022](https://www.credo.ai/news/credo-ai-and-booz-allen-hamilton-join-forces-to-bring-responsible-ai-to-the-us-federal-government)).
- **Not the mid-market** — competitor-drawn boundary: Credo fits only if you build custom ML in-house, run a 5+ person data-science team, and hold a "$30,000+/year" tooling budget; wrong if you mainly buy third-party AI ([CO-AIMS](https://co-aims.com/blog/credo-ai-review-2026-compliance-officers)) — competitor claim, but consistent with the quote-gated motion.

## 4. Why they use it

The job: **know what AI the company runs, score its risk, and produce defensible evidence before a regulator, auditor, or board asks.** The demand driver is legislative — the EU AI Act's risk-tiered obligations ([European Commission](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/policies/regulatory-framework-ai)) and US state laws turned AI inventory from nice-to-have into mandatory paperwork. A customer quote says it plainly: "accelerated our ability to be compliant with the EU AI Act by at least 10x the speed of trying to do it manually" ([vendor page](https://www.credo.ai/customers)). Alternatives define the competition: doing it by hand ("DIY with the regulation text — free but slow, the Act is 144 pages" — [HN, 2026-03-12](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=47348900)), consultants at €200–500/hr (same thread), GRC incumbents bolting AI modules onto security tooling, and focused challengers — Holistic AI, IBM watsonx.governance (partner and rival at once), deployer-side state-law tools like CO-AIMS, runtime enforcers like Execlave. Credo wins the enterprise cheque on category-first timing ("Defining AI Governance Since 2020" — vendor claim) and analyst validation.

## 5. What I would build better

1. **Publish pricing and a self-serve mid-market tier.** The demo-gate plus 404 pricing page is the same tax Vanta left open ([sibling teardown](2026-11-12-vanta.md)): CO-AIMS sells deployer-side state-law compliance at $2,388–$11,988/year ([pricing](https://co-aims.com/pricing)) to buyers Credo never touches, and Execlave publishes $199/$599 monthly tiers ([comparison](https://www.execlave.com/blog/execlave-vs-credo-ai)). A priced "registry + one framework" starter plan — metered per AI system — would harvest that tail today and feed the enterprise funnel.
2. **Rebuild the open-source funnel it abandoned.** Credo AI Lens, open-sourced with fanfare in 2022 ([Series A post](https://www.credo.ai/news/credo-ai-announces-12-8-million-series-a-funding-round-for-responsible-ai)), sits at 51 stars with its last push in June 2024 ([GitHub](https://github.com/credo-ai/credoai_lens)) — the developer channel was quietly dropped, while the HN thread shows builders doing EU AI Act work by hand because no tool speaks to them. A maintained OSS assessment/registry SDK (docs already exist) seeds exactly the registry habit the platform monetizes — the Web Security Academy playbook from the [PortSwigger teardown](2026-11-20-portswigger-burp-suite.md).
3. **Ship runtime enforcement as the product, not a research preview.** Credo's homepage argues "you cannot govern a system that acts in milliseconds with a monthly audit," yet Agent Governor is still a "Research Preview," and Execlave's whole wedge is that Credo "focus[es] on documenting what agents do rather than blocking what they can do" ([comparison](https://www.execlave.com/blog/execlave-vs-credo-ai)). Making Agent Governor generally available across the policy packs closes the flank before runtime-native challengers own the agent layer.
