# Teardown: Photopea — the one-person company that out-Photoshops the browser

> Methodological example: written to validate the method itself, kept under
> `teardowns/examples/`. It follows every rule in `docs/METHOD.md`.

- **Date analyzed:** 2026-11-05
- **Category:** B2C (with B2B self-hosting + embed API) · one-person web app
- **Model:** advertising (≈90% of revenue) + premium subscription + self-host licensing
- **Prices as seen (2026-11-05):** free with ads; Premium subscription (ad-free, 5 GB cloud, AI credits); self-hosted version $500–$2,000/month ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Photopea))
- **Revenue evidence:** verified from the founder — "almost $1 million in the last 12 months, 90% from ads," April 2021 ([Hacker News](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=26769141))

## 0. The essence (ELI9)

Photopea is a free website that works like Photoshop: you open it in your
browser and edit pictures, and your pictures never leave your computer.
Almost all of its money comes from showing ads to the millions of people who
use it for free. Some people pay a little every month to make the ads go
away. Big organizations can pay a lot to run their own private copy. One
person built it and runs the whole thing alone.

## 1. What it is, and for whom

Photopea is a full-featured photo and graphics editor that runs entirely in
the browser, built and run solo by Ivan Kutskir since 2013
([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Photopea)). It opens and saves
Photoshop's PSD plus ~40 other formats — AI, XD, Sketch, Figma, RAW, PDF —
and all processing happens locally on the user's CPU and GPU: "There are no
uploads. Photopea runs on your device" ([homepage](https://www.photopea.com/)).
The primary user is anyone who needs occasional professional-grade editing
without a Photoshop license: students, developers, freelancers, people on
Chromebooks or locked-down machines.

## 2. How it earns

Three streams, in the founder's own split
([HN, April 2021](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=26769141)): **display
ads ≈ 90%** of almost $1M in the trailing twelve months; **Premium
subscriptions** that remove ads and add 5 GB PeaDrive storage, double edit
history and ~3,000 AI credits per month
([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Photopea)); and **licensing a
self-hosted version** at $500–$2,000/month for organizations. The revenue
units are impressions (scale-driven, no login wall) plus seats for Premium
and licenses for self-hosting. Traffic is the engine: a 2021 HN commenter
cited SimilarWeb at ~8M visits per six months — a third-party estimate, but
the shape is clear: free access → massive traffic → ad revenue, with
Premium as the escape hatch for the 10% who hate ads.

## 3. Who the customers are

The evidence is unusually direct because the users explain themselves.
**Occasional professional users**: "for my personal dev projects I always
end up needing to do some light image editing. Not nearly enough to justify
a Photoshop licence" ([HN, system16](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=26769141)).
**People on restricted hardware**: "It works on any machine, including
Chromebooks, without installation"
([HN, tgsovlerkhgsel](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=26769141)).
**Photoshop-literate switchers**: "the toolbars are basically where
Photoshop's are, so you don't have to think terribly hard if you're used to
them" ([HN, dmje](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=26769141)). Press
coverage aimed at exactly this audience: "A free Photoshop alternative: No,
it's not GIMP" ([TechRepublic,
2018](https://www.techrepublic.com/article/photoshop-alternative-no-its-not-gimp/)).
The paying tail: teams and organizations that license the self-hosted
version, and premium individuals who want the ads gone.

## 4. Why they use it

The job-to-be-done: *open this one PSD (or AI, or RAW file) and edit it
properly, right now, on this machine — without buying, installing, or
re-learning anything.* The alternatives: Photoshop costs a recurring
subscription and an install; GIMP is free but users describe fighting its UX
("death by a thousand paper cuts" —
[HN thread](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=26769141)); Canva covers
templated social graphics, not layer-based editing. Photopea wins by being
instant, free, familiar to Photoshop users, and privacy-clean (files never
leave the device — a real differentiator vs. cloud editors).

## 5. What I would build better

1. **An education tier with admin controls.** Students on Chromebooks are a
   proven heavy cohort, yet the only organizational offer is self-hosting at
   $500–$2,000/month. A managed classroom plan (roster login, shared
   templates/brushes, whitelisted save destinations, district pricing) would
   convert the largest free segment into institutional revenue.
2. **A productized, priced embed API.** The API for embedding Photopea into
   other products is genuinely powerful (scripting, save-to-server) but has
   no public pricing ([API docs](https://www.photopea.com/api/)). A metered
   embed tier — branding removal, SLA, support — would formalize the
   invisible B2B usage and diversify away from ad revenue.
3. **A template/plugin marketplace with revenue share.** Templates today are
   static free files ([templates](https://www.photopea.com/templates)). A
   creator marketplace (Figma Community / Canva Creators style) adds the
   social and ecosystem layer, gives premium a reason to exist beyond ad
   removal, and costs a solo founder little in a local-first architecture.
