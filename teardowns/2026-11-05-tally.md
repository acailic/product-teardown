# Tally (tally.so) — free form builder

*Teardown date: 2026-11-05 · License: CC-BY-4.0 · All claims link to public sources.*

**Prices as seen (2026-11-05):** Free forever (unlimited forms and submissions) · Pro $24/month billed annually, $29 month-to-month · Business $74/month billed annually, $89 month-to-month — per workspace ([pricing](https://tally.so/pricing))

## The essence (for a 9-year-old)

Tally is a tool that helps people make forms — those pages on the internet where you answer questions, like "sign up here" or "tell us what you think". Making forms and collecting answers is completely free, with no limits. Some grown-ups pay a little money every month for extras, like taking Tally's small logo off their form. A couple — partners in work and life — built it in Belgium with no investors — today they and eight teammates earn a living from those small monthly payments, around $6 million a year.

## What the product is, and for whom

Tally is an online form builder launched in 2020 by founders Marie Martens and Filip Minev from Ghent, Belgium ([about page](https://tally.so/about)). Its signature choice: you build forms by typing on a blank page, like writing a Notion doc, instead of dragging boxes around ([Show HN, 2021](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=28127746): "We set off building Tally — a new type of form builder for makers and no-coders"). It counts 2.5 million users as of September 2026 ([$6M ARR post](https://blog.tally.so/6-years-in-6-million-far/)). The company is deliberately small, independent and bootstrapped: "Bootstrapped and customer-funded. Building for the long run" ([about](https://tally.so/about)).

## How it makes money

**Model:** freemium subscriptions, with an unusually generous free tier — unlimited forms *and* unlimited submissions, including conditional logic, payments, signatures and file uploads, free forever ([pricing page](https://tally.so/pricing)).

**Prices / revenue unit:** the revenue unit is a monthly subscription per workspace: **Pro at $24/month billed annually ($29 month-to-month)** (remove "Made with Tally" branding, custom domains, team collaboration, partial submissions, custom CSS, drop-off analytics) and **Business at $74/month billed annually ($89 month-to-month)** (data-retention controls, email verification, 90-day version history) ([pricing page](https://tally.so/pricing)).

**Scale, with public proof:** Tally publishes a complete MRR timeline on its blog: $1K MRR (Mar 2021) → $100K (Feb 2024) → $258K (Jun 2025) → **$501K MRR / $6M ARR (Sep 2026), with 2.5M users and a team of 10** ([6-year recap](https://blog.tally.so/6-years-in-6-million-far/), [growth history](https://blog.tally.so/how-we-grew-tally-to-4m-arr-fully-bootstrapped/)). Conversion is thin by design: only ~1% of users who sign up via the viral "Made with Tally" badge eventually upgrade ([6-year recap](https://blog.tally.so/6-years-in-6-million-far/)). Acquisition has shifted: 43% of new users now arrive via ChatGPT/Claude/Gemini/Perplexity recommendations, overtaking the badge loop ([6-year recap](https://blog.tally.so/6-years-in-6-million-far/)).

## Who the customers are

- **Indie hackers, no-coders, creators, small teams** — Tally says it explicitly: "We built Tally for: People who value great UX and design; Builders who get excited by tools like Notion; No-code makers and indie hackers" ([$4M ARR post](https://blog.tally.so/how-we-grew-tally-to-4m-arr-fully-bootstrapped/)). A Redditor's joke they endorse: "your average stuffy SME owner will have no idea how this notepad-looking thing is a form builder."
- **Community & event organizers** — an HN user surveyed a 68K-member Discord community with it ("Coming from Google Forms, I really appreciate the modern UX"); another ran 300+ attendee event registrations, fleeing Typeform pricing ([Show HN 2022](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=30258149)).
- **Startups/agencies** — homepage testimonials feature the founder of Dub.co (ex-Vercel) and teams "scaling global teams in Web3" ([homepage](https://tally.so/)).

## Why they use it instead of alternatives

- **vs Typeform:** Typeform's free tier allows 10 responses/month — "not sufficient for any size of business" (Tally founder, [Show HN 2022](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=30258149)); paid plans were "an arm and a leg" and "not friendly to bootstrappers" (users, same thread). Tally's counter-position: "unlimited forms and submissions for free… paywalls getting in the way? Not anymore" ([pricing](https://tally.so/pricing)).
- **vs Google Forms:** users switch for modern UX, Notion-like editing and built-in integrations ([HN testimonial](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=30258149)).
- **Job-to-be-done:** "make a good-looking form in seconds, free, with no paywall ambush." The product itself is the growth engine: most people discover Tally by *filling out* a Tally form, then the badge converts creators ([$4M post](https://blog.tally.so/how-we-grew-tally-to-4m-arr-fully-bootstrapped/)) — today amplified by AI assistants recommending the free tier.

## What I would build better

1. **Consent-first partial submissions.** Tally's Pro feature captures answers typed but never submitted; HN users called it "fundamentally unethical" and a GDPR problem — one wrote it "stops me using it… in a corporate capacity" ([thread](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=30258149)). A "save draft" model with explicit respondent consent keeps the conversion-analytics value while removing the backlash — and it matters more as Tally courts organizations.
2. **Trust posture ahead of scale.** The August 2026 breach exposed emails + hashed passwords via a Metabase zero-day — data that "didn't need to be accessible through Metabase in the first place" ([Tell HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49213500), [6-year recap](https://blog.tally.so/6-years-in-6-million-far/)). I'd ship data-minimization (credentials never reach analytics), SOC 2/ISO 27001 (now only "expected by end of 2026"), and a self-host/export option for CTOs who refuse closed-source form tools ([HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=30258149)) — before growth makes them mandatory.
3. **Own the AI front door.** 43% of signups come from LLM recommendations the company admits it "can't buy" and could lose "overnight" on a model update ([6-year recap](https://blog.tally.so/6-years-in-6-million-far/)). I'd invest harder in machine-readable docs, the MCP/API surface (already live, 1M+ AI calls/month) and per-model visibility — that channel is now the acquisition moat, and it's rented land.
