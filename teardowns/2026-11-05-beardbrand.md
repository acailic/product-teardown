# Teardown: Beardbrand

**Date:** 2026-11-05 · **Type:** direct-to-consumer men's grooming (Shopify) · **Founded:** 2012 · **HQ:** Austin, TX · **Ownership:** bootstrapped, private — current revenue not published

## The essence (for a 9-year-old)

Beardbrand sells soap, oil, and cologne made just for men with beards. It became famous because its founder made hundreds of YouTube videos teaching men how to take care of their beards. People who watched the videos learned to trust the company, and then they bought its products — even though they cost more than the cheap bottles in a supermarket. The beard itself is the real product: Beardbrand sells men a way to feel proud and stylish wearing one.

## 1. What is the product, and for whom?

The catalog spans beard care (oil, balm, wash, softener), hair styling (paste, sea salt spray), body (bar soap, deodorant), cologne, and tools such as a trimmer and combs ([store navigation](https://www.beardbrand.com/collections)). Founder Eric Bandholz targets what he calls **"Urban Beardsmen"** — men who care "not only [about] their facial hair but their style, their careers, their independence." After meeting white-collar professionals at a 2012 Portland beard competition, he built a community (blog, Tumblr, YouTube) *before* selling anything ([Mixergy interview](https://mixergy.com/interviews/eric-bandholz-beardbrand-interview/)). The store launched on Shopify with 2–3 products, got an early New York Times mention, started by reselling other brands' products, and only later formulated its own — reaching "just under 20 employees" with shipping from the US and UK ([Mixergy](https://mixergy.com/interviews/eric-bandholz-beardbrand-interview/), [Failory interview](https://failory.com/mistakes/beardbrand)).

## 2. How does it make money?

Classic DTC e-commerce: customers buy bottles directly from beardbrand.com — no retail middlemen.

- **Unit prices:** Beard Oil costs **$29–$32** for 1.7 oz (50 ml); a five-fragrance oil set starts at **$122** ([beard oil collection](https://www.beardbrand.com/collections/beard-oil), fetched 2026-11-05).
- **Basket nudges:** free US shipping over $75 ([product page](https://www.beardbrand.com/products/tree-ranger-beard-oil)).
- **Paid community:** a one-time **$90 Alliance Membership** buys a private community plus free standard US shipping; it is also granted free after three orders or a single $150 order ([Alliance page](https://www.beardbrand.com/products/alliance-membership)).
- **Revenue: unknown** (private company). The public trail: **$76.5k in one month** (Nov 2013), self-posted by Bandholz on Reddit ([quoted on Mixergy](https://mixergy.com/interviews/eric-bandholz-beardbrand-interview/)); a **"7 figure business"** by 2018 ([Failory](https://failory.com/mistakes/beardbrand)); grown "from a $30 investment to a seven figure business" on organic content ([SplitBase podcast](https://splitbase.com/podcast/eric-bandholz-ep13)); an interviewer's title claims **"$50M+ in revenue"** cumulatively ([LinkedIn](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/mitchelllandoncohen_ive-interviewed-50-founders-this-was-activity-7409261611091173376-4Lyu)); and in June 2026 Bandholz said revenue **"is down from its peak"** after 15 years ([Practical Ecommerce](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=CvLTCOKjHpo)).

## 3. Who are the customers?

The core segment is the Urban Beardsman: design- and finance-type professionals who reject the lumberjack/biker stereotype ([Mixergy](https://mixergy.com/interviews/eric-bandholz-beardbrand-interview/)). Acquisition is content-led — a customer survey found **46% first heard of Beardbrand via social media**, with YouTube first, then Facebook, then Reddit ([Mixergy](https://mixergy.com/interviews/eric-bandholz-beardbrand-interview/)); a Trustpilot review is literally titled "Found through a YouTube video" ([Trustpilot](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/beardbrand.com)). Storefront review widgets rate the oils **4.6–4.8/5** ([collection page](https://www.beardbrand.com/collections/beard-oil)); Trustpilot shows 4.1/5 overall but with only two reviews in the last 12 months, so on-site reviews are the better demand signal ([Trustpilot](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/beardbrand.com)).

## 4. Why do they use it instead of alternatives?

The job-to-be-done is not "buy oil" but "keep a beard worth being proud of": stop itch and beardruff, soften the hair, and smell like "you just hiked through a Pacific Northwest Forest" ([product copy](https://www.beardbrand.com/products/tree-ranger-beard-oil)). The deeper job is identity — looking like a refined, independent man rather than a caveman. Concrete alternatives: generic beard oils crowding Amazon ([search "beard oil"](https://www.amazon.com/s?k=beard+oil), typically far below $29), ordinary hair products, or doing nothing. Beardbrand wins on **trust built before the sale** — a 2.14M-subscriber YouTube channel of grooming tutorials ([@Beardbrand](https://www.youtube.com/@Beardbrand), 2,140,000 followers checked 2026-11-05) — plus free fragrance swaps ([storefront](https://www.beardbrand.com/collections/beard-oil)). The moat is the brand, not the chemistry: Bandholz himself says beard care has gone from "a blue ocean… to a red one of fierce competition" ([Practical Ecommerce, 2026](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=CvLTCOKjHpo)).

## 5. What I would build better

1. **A real starter kit (~$15–20).** The #1 acquisition channel (YouTube) recruits first-time beard growers, yet the cheapest entry is a $29+ full-size bottle, and the category's cheap shelf undercuts that. Travel-size oil + wash + balm bundles would convert curious viewers at low risk — the classic move when growth has plateaued and every new customer is contested ([plateau source](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=CvLTCOKjHpo)).
2. **A visible replenishment subscription.** Oils and soaps run out, but as of 2026-11-05 no subscribe-and-save option appears on any fetched product page. Recurring orders plus the existing $90 Alliance community would compound customer lifetime value exactly while acquisition gets harder.
3. **Frictionless international checkout.** Every order ships from the US ([product page](https://www.beardbrand.com/products/tree-ranger-beard-oil)) while the content audience is global; Bandholz is already pushing cross-border selling via OpenBorder toward a possible EU warehouse ([Marketers Index](https://marketersindex.com/beardbrand-navigates-market-shift-and-strategic-pivot-after-revenue-plateau/)). Making duties-included, landed-cost checkout a first-class experience is the highest-leverage product work behind that strategy.
