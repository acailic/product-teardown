# Remove.bg — Product Teardown (2026-11-05)

## The essence (for a 9-year-old)

Remove.bg is a website that cuts a person or object out of a photo in about five seconds — the boring job that used to take a trained person many minutes with expensive software. You can try it for free and see a small preview; to download the nice big version you pay with "credits," like tokens at an arcade. Shops that sell things online and photographers who handle thousands of photos loved it so much that over 30 million people used it every month. A bigger company called Canva bought it in 2021 — and has now decided to switch the website off (December 1, 2026) and keep only the part that works inside Canva.

## What the product is, and for whom

Remove.bg is a one-click, AI-powered background-removal service: upload an image, get back a cut-out with a transparent background, "100% automatically – in 5 seconds – without a single click" ([remove.bg](https://www.remove.bg/)). It ships as a website, a developer API, and plugins for Photoshop, Windows/Mac/Linux and Android ([tools & API](https://www.remove.bg/tools-api)). It was created in 2018 by Kaleido AI GmbH, a bootstrapped company founded by Benjamin Groessing and David Fankhauser in Vienna, Austria ([Kaleido press release, Feb 2021](http://web.archive.org/web/20210224104422/https://www.kaleido.ai/press.html)). Its audience spans individuals, e-commerce sellers, professional photographers, developers embedding the API, and enterprises doing bulk processing ([About](https://www.remove.bg/about-us)).

## How it earns (model, prices, revenue unit)

Freemium **credit** model — the revenue unit is one credit ≈ one full-resolution (HD) download; small previews are free, and 1 HD image costs 1 credit ([archived pricing page, June 2026](http://web.archive.org/web/20260604125054/https://www.remove.bg/pricing)):

- **Subscriptions:** Personal $9/month ($108/year); Professional from $39 to $589/month; Business from $789 to $5,450/month ([archived pricing](http://web.archive.org/web/20260604125054/https://www.remove.bg/pricing)).
- **Pay-as-you-go credit packs:** from $3 for 3 credits (~$1/image) down to $1,699 for 8,000 credits (~$0.21/image) ([archived pricing](http://web.archive.org/web/20260604125054/https://www.remove.bg/pricing)).
- **Developer API** sold on the same credit system ([API](https://www.remove.bg/api)).

Total revenue is **unknown** — remove.bg is a private Canva subsidiary and publishes no financials; the acquisition price was never disclosed. Best public signals: bootstrapped (they "declined numerous opportunities for investment") and 25 employees at acquisition ([press release](http://web.archive.org/web/20210224104422/https://www.kaleido.ai/press.html)); 100M+ people used Kaleido's products in 2020 ([press release](http://web.archive.org/web/20210224104422/https://www.kaleido.ai/press.html)); today it processes 150M+ images/month for 32M+ monthly active users and 240+ enterprise clients ([About](https://www.remove.bg/about-us)). Note: the standalone site shuts down **1 December 2026**, with subscriptions pro-rated/refunded and PAYG credits handled at shutdown ([official FAQ](https://www.remove.bg/faq)).

## Who the customers are

- **Marketplaces and resale e-commerce** — Vestiaire Collective halved photo-processing costs; Thriftify grew 5x; TheNextCloset raised conversion ([success stories](https://www.remove.bg/success_stories)).
- **Photography studios at volume** — FOX-MAR saved 75% of background-removal time; Kemmetmueller Photography saved 12.5 weeks of processing; Glossy Finish processed 4,000 photos in a day ([success stories](https://www.remove.bg/success_stories)).
- **Print-on-demand and developers** — Printful reported 90% design approval; API customers like BAXS and Pixelcando ([success stories](https://www.remove.bg/success_stories)).
- **Car dealerships and enterprise bulk processing** — e.g. car-auction company Aston Barclay ([success stories](https://www.remove.bg/success_stories); [segment page](https://www.remove.bg/g/car-dealerships)); 240+ enterprise clients overall ([About](https://www.remove.bg/about-us)).
- **Casual individuals** — the 32M MAU who use free previews for profile pictures and fun edits ([About](https://www.remove.bg/about-us)).

## Why they use it instead of alternatives

The job-to-be-done is *"get thousands of cut-outs fast, without a photo-editing expert."* Before remove.bg, the alternatives were manual clipping paths in Photoshop (minutes of skilled work per image) or human outsourcing services like Pixelz at ~$0.60/image — remove.bg won on instant turnaround and scale, which launch-era commenters called its decisive edge ([HN, 2019](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=19418244)). Marketplaces also need uniform white backgrounds across thousands of listings — a consistency job automation does best ([success stories](https://www.remove.bg/success_stories)). The moat was never the model itself: by 2023, competitors attacked remove.bg directly on "annoying subscriptions" and price ([HN, 2023](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=36064639)), and by 2026 open-source clones claim equal quality at ~1/40th the price ([HN, 2026](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=47897825)).

## What I would build better

1. **An API-first product with a real longevity promise.** Canva is switching off a service with 32M MAU and 240+ enterprise clients to fold it into a design suite ([FAQ](https://www.remove.bg/faq)) — leaving API customers who built pipelines scrambling for alternatives. Serving *integrations*, not end-users, is the defensible position: enterprises pay for uptime, SLAs and migration guarantees, not for a prettier website.
2. **Dead-simple pay-per-image pricing.** Credit tiers, monthly resets and app-vs-web price differences bred exactly the resentment competitors exploited with "without annoying subscriptions" pitches ([HN, 2023](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=36064639)). One transparent price with volume discounts would remove the #1 stated reason to switch.
3. **Local/on-device batch processing as the free tier.** Open-source models already match the quality ([HN, 2026](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=47897825)); a free offline desktop batch tool would own the long tail (privacy-sensitive users, bulk hobbyists) and leave paid cloud for API automation and enterprise workflows.
