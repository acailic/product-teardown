# Fathom — AI meeting notetaker (teardown, 2026-11-05)

Subject: **Fathom**, the AI meeting notetaker at [fathom.ai](https://www.fathom.ai/) (formerly fathom.video) — not to be confused with Fathom Analytics (web analytics) or Fathom HQ (financial reporting).

## The essence (for a 9-year-old)

Fathom is a robot helper that joins your video meetings and writes the notes for you. While people talk, it listens and remembers everything; right after the call it hands you a short summary and a list of who promised to do what. One person can use it free forever; companies pay a few dollars every month for each teammate who uses it. The team became so good at this that Superhuman, a bigger company that makes a fancy email app, bought all of Fathom in September 2026 ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/09/14/superhuman-acquires-yc-backed-notetaker-fathom-as-productivity-platforms-push-for-agentic-work/)).

## What the product is and who it's for

Fathom records, transcribes and summarizes Zoom, Google Meet and Teams calls — in classic "bot" mode or a bot-free mode — then pushes summaries, action items and CRM updates into HubSpot, Salesforce, Slack and more ([YC company page](https://www.ycombinator.com/companies/fathom), [homepage](https://www.fathom.ai/)). Founded 2020, YC Winter 2021, team of 95 ([YC](https://www.ycombinator.com/companies/fathom)). By September 2026: 400,000+ monthly active users, and 1M+ people had recorded a meeting ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/09/14/superhuman-acquires-yc-backed-notetaker-fathom-as-productivity-platforms-push-for-agentic-work/)). The homepage sells to sales, customer success, marketing, ops, HR and product teams — but the front door is the single free user.

## How it earns money

Freemium SaaS. The free tier is famously generous (unlimited recordings, transcriptions, instant summaries — [pricing page](https://www.fathom.ai/pricing)); above that it sells per-user-per-month subscriptions, so **the unit of revenue is the paid seat**:

- Free — $0 (unlimited recording, transcription, instant AI summaries)
- Premium — $16/user/mo billed annually, $20 monthly
- Team — $15/user/mo billed annually, $19 monthly (2-user minimum), adds shared workspace, HIPAA
- Business — $25/user/mo billed annually, $34 monthly, adds CRM field sync, deal view, coaching metrics
- Enterprise — $35/user/mo billed annually, adds SSO/SCIM, Launch Assist implementation

G2's independent pricing snapshot agrees ("Free; Premium $16.00; Team starting at $15.00") ([G2, Sept 2026 snapshot](http://web.archive.org/web/20260901120306/https://www.g2.com/products/fathom-video/reviews)).

Actual revenue is **unknown** (private company). Best public signals: revenue grew ~90x between 2022 and 2024 ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2024/09/19/ai-notetaker-fathom-raises-17m/)); hiring posts claimed 10–20% monthly ARR + usage growth ([HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=44434765)); PitchBook put the 2024 valuation at $94M with >$30M raised ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/09/14/superhuman-acquires-yc-backed-notetaker-fathom-as-productivity-platforms-push-for-agentic-work/)). Telling detail: $2M of the $17M Series A came from Fathom's own users via Wefunder crowdfunding ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2024/09/19/ai-notetaker-fathom-raises-17m/)).

## Who the customers are

G2 shows **9.9/10 from 7,016 reviews** ([G2 snapshot, Sept 2026](http://web.archive.org/web/20260901120306/https://www.g2.com/products/fathom-video/reviews)). G2's own reviewer-filter counts: mid-market 1,373 and enterprise 154 — so roughly 5,500 (about 78%) of reviewers are in companies of ≤50 people (derived from those counts). Top named industries: computer software (914), IT & services (613), professional training & coaching (310), plus a 3,479-strong "Other" long tail; regions: North America 4,166, Europe 1,208 ([G2](http://web.archive.org/web/20260901120306/https://www.g2.com/products/fathom-video/reviews)). Reviewer roles in current top reviews: sales operations coordinator, associate Salesforce administrator ([G2](http://web.archive.org/web/20260901120306/https://www.g2.com/products/fathom-video/reviews)). The HubSpot integration alone served 8,500+ companies by 2024 ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2024/09/19/ai-notetaker-fathom-raises-17m/)), and Fathom describes itself as the most-installed app on the HubSpot marketplace ([HN hiring post](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48360712)).

## Why they use it instead of alternatives

The job-to-be-done: *be fully present in the conversation, and never lose a decision or a follow-up.* Named rivals on G2's own comparison widget: Otter.ai (4.4/5), Fireflies.ai (4.7/5), Gong; the market also includes Read AI, Granola and the free recorders built into Zoom/Meet/Teams ([G2](http://web.archive.org/web/20260901120306/https://www.g2.com/products/fathom-video/reviews), [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/09/14/superhuman-acquires-yc-backed-notetaker-fathom-as-productivity-platforms-push-for-agentic-work/)). What users pick Fathom for:

1. **A free tier that actually covers the whole job** — unlimited recording and summaries at $0 ([pricing](https://www.fathom.ai/pricing)), which is the distribution engine behind those 8,500 HubSpot companies.
2. **Summary quality and flexibility** — TechCrunch's reviewer found Otter shoehorns every meeting into "a somewhat rigid flow… mostly geared toward users in sales," while Fathom adapts chapters to the meeting type ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2024/09/19/ai-notetaker-fathom-raises-17m/)).
3. **Less friction and fear** — one reviewer chose it over Fireflies precisely to avoid "another login to a tool they'll never use"; the April 2026 bot-free mode answers the complaint that "everyone has their own meeting assistant" crowding the room ([G2](http://web.archive.org/web/20260901120306/https://www.g2.com/products/fathom-video/reviews), [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/04/15/fathom-adds-a-bot-less-meeting-mode-in-a-bid-to-take-on-granola/)).

## What I would build better

1. **A trust layer for the other side of the call.** The category's deepest pain sits with the *recorded* person: bot fatigue and consent anxiety. Build visible consent receipts and two-sided summaries ("both sides leave with the same notes"), not just quiet capture. Why top-of-list: the bot-crowding backlash and privacy-first rivals like Granola (which raised $125M at a $1.5B valuation) show that whoever makes the customer comfortable removes the adopter's biggest fear ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/04/15/fathom-adds-a-bot-less-meeting-mode-in-a-bid-to-take-on-granola/), [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/03/25/granola-raises-125m-hits-1-5b-valuation-as-it-expands-from-meeting-notetaker-to-enterprise-ai-app/)).
2. **Close the loop, not just the note.** Base models are commoditizing summaries; durable value moves to *verified follow-through* — action items tracked to completion, CRM fields updated as proof, follow-up emails actually sent. Superhuman bought Fathom exactly to route meeting outcomes into agents ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/09/14/superhuman-acquires-yc-backed-notetaker-fathom-as-productivity-platforms-push-for-agentic-work/)); a builder should own "the meeting that finishes itself."
3. **A cross-meeting pattern radar, built transparently.** Fathom's CEO already imagines alerting leadership when a competitor's name spreads across calls ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2024/09/19/ai-notetaker-fathom-raises-17m/)). Ship it with employee-visible rules and opt-ins — otherwise it reads as surveillance and dies in procurement.
