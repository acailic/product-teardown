# Teardown: iubenda — legal compliance as a metered utility for websites

- **Date analyzed:** 2026-11-09
- **Category:** B2B/B2C · compliance SaaS (privacy, consent, accessibility)
- **Model:** per-site subscription + usage meters (pageviews, stored consents); free plan; bootstrapped start → acquired, now consolidator
- **Prices as seen (2026-11-09):** Core suite per site/month billed yearly: Essentials €4.99 (€5.99 monthly, 25K pageviews) · Advanced €19.99 (€21.99 monthly, 50K pageviews) · Ultimate €79.99 (€89.99 monthly, 150K pageviews); overage €0.05 per 1,000 pageviews; USD $5.99–$99.99 ([pricing](https://www.iubenda.com/en/pricing))
- **Revenue evidence:** **estimated** — no public revenue figures; verified: founded 2011, joined team.blue 2022, parent of three CMP brands ([about](https://www.iubenda.com/en/about-iubenda/), [CookieFirst](https://cookiefirst.com/cookiefirst-joins-iubenda-what-this-means-for-you/), [Complianz](https://complianz.io/), [consentmanager](https://www.consentmanager.net/))

## 0. The essence (ELI9)

iubenda rents websites their legal safety equipment. If you run a website, the law says you must tell visitors what you do with their data and ask before planting tracking cookies on their computer. Doing that properly means writing legal documents that change every time the law does — expensive if you hire a lawyer, dangerous if you copy a template. iubenda generates the documents and the "accept cookies?" popup, keeps both automatically updated as laws change, and charges a small monthly rent per website — a bit more as the site gets busier. One Italian founder started it in 2011; today more than 150,000 businesses use it (vendor claim), a giant European hosting family owns it, and it has bought three competitors.

## 1. What it is, and for whom

iubenda is a digital-compliance platform: a cookie-consent banner (CMP), a privacy/cookie-policy generator built from a 2,400+-clause library, terms-and-conditions generation, a consent-proof database, and an AI accessibility widget, sold as one suite with geo-targeted consent flows ([pricing](https://www.iubenda.com/en/pricing)). Advanced Solutions cover data-subject requests and whistleblowing reports, and the nav markets compliance "by regulation" — EU GDPR, ePrivacy, US state laws, LGPD, FADP, the EU Whistleblowing Directive ([site](https://www.iubenda.com/en/)). Founded in 2011 by Andrea Giannangelo; iubenda S.r.l. is registered in Milan (share capital €12,795.78 fully paid); in 2022 it joined team.blue, and it is now "parent company of consentmanager, CookieFirst and Complianz" ([about](https://www.iubenda.com/en/about-iubenda/)). The primary user is the owner, marketer, or agency running a business website that falls under privacy law — practically any site with EU visitors.

## 2. How it earns

**Per-site subscription with usage meters** ([pricing, seen 2026-11-09](https://www.iubenda.com/en/pricing)):

- **Core compliance solutions:** Essentials €4.99/site/mo billed yearly (€5.99 monthly), 25K pageviews; Advanced €19.99 (€21.99 monthly), 50K pageviews, "most popular"; Ultimate €79.99 (€89.99 monthly), 150K pageviews, enterprise-grade. The revenue unit is the **site**, expanded by the **pageview meter**: €0.05 per 1,000 above quota — a natural upsell as traffic grows.
- Add-ons sold separately: an AI accessibility widget (Lite €6/site/mo, Standard €50–55 EUR / $60–65 USD) and a **Consent Database at €5/mo per 1,000 stored consents** ([pricing](https://www.iubenda.com/en/pricing)).
- A free plan funnels in generator users ("Start with the free plan"); an agency program targets multi-client buyers ([pricing](https://www.iubenda.com/en/pricing)).

**Revenue: estimated** — iubenda discloses none. Vendor scale claims: 150,000+ clients, 2.5M+ generated documents, 7+ billion pages/month running its banner, 100+ countries ([about](https://www.iubenda.com/en/about-iubenda/)). Verified anchors around it: team.blue (4,000+ employees, 3.4M SMB customers, €850M+ EOY25 ARR across its brands) provides distribution to millions of hosted domains ([team.blue](https://team.blue/)); iubenda is now itself the acquirer of three CMP brands ([CookieFirst, 2025](https://cookiefirst.com/cookiefirst-joins-iubenda-what-this-means-for-you/), [Complianz](https://complianz.io/), [consentmanager](https://www.consentmanager.net/)).

## 3. Who the customers are

- **SMB website owners and e-commerce operators** — the core segment, evidenced by the €4.99 entry point, per-site billing, and "small businesses" positioning ([pricing](https://www.iubenda.com/en/pricing)).
- **Agencies building client sites** — the WordPress long tail is third-party-visible: the iubenda plugin has [200,000+ active installs, 4.5/5 from 398 ratings](https://wordpress.org/plugins/iubenda/), and acquired Complianz has [1,000,000+ active installs](https://wordpress.org/plugins/complianz-gdpr-cookie-consent/) on wordpress.org.
- **Higher-traffic publishers/SaaS** — Ultimate's 150K-pageview tier and geo-targeting ([pricing](https://www.iubenda.com/en/pricing)).
- **team.blue's hosting customer base** — post-acquisition distribution across the group's 10.6M active domains ([team.blue](https://team.blue/)).

## 4. Why they use it

The job: **keep a website legally compliant continuously, without a lawyer on retainer**. Privacy law changes (GDPR, ePrivacy, US state laws), and each new tracking script a site adds changes what the policy must disclose. iubenda's promise is self-updating documents — 2,400+ maintained clauses — plus a banner that blocks scripts until visitors consent ([pricing](https://www.iubenda.com/en/pricing)). The alternative is a static template that quietly goes stale, the "high cost of custom legal services" ([about](https://www.iubenda.com/en/about-iubenda/)), or enterprise CMPs like OneTrust built for a different buyer. Rivals at the same price band: Cookiebot, Termly ([its own HN launch](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=17608530)), CookieYes — while consolidation already absorbed three others: consentmanager, CookieFirst and Complianz. Users stay because ripping out a consent banner and regenerating policies is risky, boring work; churn has to justify itself against a €5–20/mo line item.

## 5. What I would build better

1. **Sell proof, not paperwork — an "audit pack" SKU.** iubenda sells compliance *artifacts*; when a data-protection authority or an enterprise client asks "show me consent was properly collected," the owner needs *evidence*. The pieces exist (Consent Database, monthly site scans, document versioning) but no pricing-page SKU bundles them into a signed, regulator-ready export ([pricing, seen 2026-11-09](https://www.iubenda.com/en/pricing)). This converts a €5/1,000-consents storage meter into compliance insurance — the artifact buyers under regulatory pressure actually pay for.
2. **Decouple delivery from iubenda's CDN.** Banners and policies are served from iubenda's infrastructure; in 2022 a customer's site started opening windows on every click, traced to a [cdn.iubenda.com script — "Malware Injection in Iubenda?"](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=31315212), and rivals' embed-only model draws the same [dependency complaints](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=17608530). A self-hostable/static export with integrity signing would sell trust — a compliance vendor that can take your legal pages down with it is a liability DPOs notice.
3. **Portfolio pricing for agencies.** Billing meters pageviews per site (25K–150K quotas, €0.05/1K overage), so an agency running 30 client sites faces 30 bills and unpredictable overages; the pricing page courts agencies with a heading but no pooled tier ([pricing](https://www.iubenda.com/en/pricing)). A flat multi-site plan would lock in the exact partners who today install Complianz across a million WordPress sites.
