# Teardown methodology

Every teardown in [teardowns/](../teardowns/) is one product or small business, analyzed the same way: explain the essence so a 9-year-old could follow it, then answer four questions with public evidence — how it earns, who the customers are, why they use it instead of alternatives, and what we would build better.

## Format

Each teardown answers the questions in this order:

1. **The essence (for a 9-year-old)** — 3–5 jargon-free sentences, at the top.
2. **What the product is, and for whom** — one short section: what it does, launch year, scale, and the audience.
3. **How it earns** — model, prices, revenue unit, with public evidence for each claim; unknown revenue is marked unknown rather than estimated.
4. **Who the customers are** — real customer quotes or behavior, sourced from reviews and forums.
5. **Why they use it instead of alternatives** — names concrete alternatives and states the job-to-be-done.
6. **What we would build better** — numbered improvements, each with the reason it matters first.

## Constraints

- Every claim carries a public link to its source.
- Free public sources only.
- English, 500–900 words per teardown.
